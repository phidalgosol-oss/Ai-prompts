The four-week cycle

The week above repeats, but its centre of gravity moves. One full turn takes a month and ends with proof she can show.

Week 1 · now

Warm strike

  • 10 personal invites to her own network
  • Offer approved, templates ready
  • Comments before asks
Exit: 10 contacted
Week 2

Rooms she's in

  • Contribute in import + founder communities
  • First 5 verified nano/micro names
  • First guest pitches land
Exit: 5 community-sourced testers
Week 3

Listen hard

  • Tester sessions run in volume
  • Feature requests ranked
  • Language DB hits 30 verbatims
Exit: feedback → roadmap
Week 4

Turn them loud

  • Close the loop with every tester
  • Ask for quotes, intros, collabs
  • Month review: what moved, what dies
Exit: first consented social proof

  • Warm tester invites10 max

Rules that decide the close calls

  • Two useful answers before one ask. Contribute in a community before recruiting from it.
  • Publish only when she can stay. Thirty minutes of real replies beats a perfect caption — early conversation depth is the ranking signal that matters now.
  • Three good beats seven mediocre. Daily filler can mark the account as low-engagement.
  • Nano before macro. 1–10k creators reply to cold outreach at 20–30%; 1M accounts don't.
  • @prihisol never sells. Not the course, not DxVida. Its job is trust.
  • No invented numbers, ever. Unknown stays unknown until she measures it.
Evidence behind the rules: Instagram 2026 ranking signals — shares, saves, early conversation velocity — Later, Sprout Social, Mirra. Creator tiers and response rates — Digital Applied, InfluenceFlow. Tester sourcing split and cohort sizing — Usersnap, Eleken.
